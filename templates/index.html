FAQ

Common questions

What is an ATS and why does it matter?
An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that companies use to automatically filter resumes before a human recruiter sees them. A 2021 Harvard Business School study found that 88% of employers say qualified candidates are screened out by ATS because their resume doesn't match the exact search terms — not because they lack the skills. Your ATS score tells you how likely your resume is to pass this filter.
Is my resume stored or shared?
No. Your resume PDF is deleted from our server immediately after the analysis completes. We do not store, share, or use your resume for any other purpose. No account is required and no personal data is collected.
What makes this different from just counting keywords?
Most ATS checkers simply count how many words from the JD appear in your resume. ResumeIQ uses AI semantic matching — it understands that "DSA" means "data structures", that "ML" means "machine learning", and that "built a Python tool" signals "Python development". Our skill normalization graph maps 150+ informal student phrases to the exact terms Indian recruiters search for.
What score should I aim for?
Aim for 70 or above for roles you are applying to. A score above 75 means strong keyword and semantic alignment. Below 50 means your resume needs significant keyword work before applying. Remember — the score is a guide, not a guarantee. A high score means your resume is more likely to reach a human recruiter.
Why is the analysis taking 15–30 seconds?
The AI semantic analysis uses a sentence-transformer model (MiniLM) that runs on CPU. It typically takes 15–30 seconds to compute semantic similarity across your resume sections and the job description. This is normal and unavoidable for a genuine AI analysis. Simple keyword counters are faster but much less accurate.
